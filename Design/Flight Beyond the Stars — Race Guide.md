# Flight Beyond the Stars — Race Guide

Nov 6, 2026 · @Mark

## Overview

Eight species can crew the player's ship: humans plus seven major alien races. Each has a distinct look, voice, set of values, crew strengths and a role in the story; the best specialists for any station come from outside humanity.

**Crew skill caps by species** (0–250 scale; first-pass numbers for balancing; the highest cap per role is marked in bold)

**Starting crew and unlocks.** In a standard game the captain and all starting crew are human. Every other species must be earned (quests, rescues, reputation, story) or bought (recruitment halls, Orrun brokers). **New Game+** unlocks every species as starting crew and captain.

**Relationships at the start of the game**

- The **Orrun Combine** trade with everyone and are trusted by no one.
- The **Varkesh** respect the **Thuum** for their endurance and protect them; they despise the **Skarrow** as thieves.
- The **Noeti** and **Ophane** share an ancient rivalry over whose records of the past are true.
- The **Murmur** unsettle everyone, and only the Noeti study them without fear.
- The **Ophane** shun all outsiders; the **Skarrow** consider their borders a dare.
- **Humans** are a young, well-liked species — until the truth about the Firstborn spreads.

## Humans

The player's own species: adaptable generalists who believe they are a young people native to this galaxy, and are wrong.

- **Look:** as we are, with a wide range of cultures and styles across the home region.
- **Voice:** varied; the default "everyman" register the player reads other races against.
- **Values:** exploration, independence, getting ahead. **Offended by:** being told they don't belong.
- **Crew trait — Adaptable:** gain experience 25% faster and suffer no morale clash with any species.
- **Homeworld:** **Haven** — named by the Firstborn, who hoped it would be exactly that. A quiet dramatic irony for Act 2.
- **Trade:** balanced; the home starport sells a bit of everything at fair prices.
- **Story role:** the inheritors of the Firstborn's secret. As the truth spreads, other races' attitudes toward human captains shift, and the hidden Firstborn society works inside human institutions.
- **Notable character:** **Commander Ada Reyes**, starport chief and the player's first employer; brisk, warm, and secretly a member of the Firstborn society.

## Noeti

An ancient scholar race of patient, brilliant researchers with weak fleets — they remember humanity's arrival and have kept quiet about it for millennia.

- **Look:** slender and tall, with long six-fingered hands and a crest of soft bioluminescent patterns that shift as they think; part of their language is light.
- **Voice:** precise, gently wry, fond of footnotes and of answering a question with a better question. *"An excellent question. Incorrect, but excellent."*
- **Values:** knowledge, patience, preservation. **Offended by:** lies, haste, destroying artifacts.
- **Preferred posture:** Curious (the suggested new posture); Hostile ends conversations for a long time.
- **Trade:** buy artifacts, specimens and survey data at the best prices in the galaxy; sell sensors, science tech and translation databases.
- **Combat:** few ships, rarely seen, with superb shields and sensors but light weapons.
- **Crew trait — Archivist:** identifies any artifact on pickup and speeds translation of new languages.
- **Homeworld:** **Silpon Prime**, named for Silpon, their legendary founding scientist (a nod to Master of Orion's research race).
- **Story role:** primary Act 2 source; they hold a sealed account of the Firstborn's arrival and guard a Firstborn vault.
- **Notable character:** **Archivist Ilun-Vey**, impossibly old, kind, and carrying centuries of guilt for staying silent.

## Orrun Combine

A cheerful, relentlessly commercial merchant civilization that trades with everyone, sells anything — including information about the player — and runs the galaxy's biggest frontier station.

- **Look:** stout, barrel-bodied and four-armed, with color-shifting throat sacs that swell when they bargain. Dress in layered, pocket-covered coats.
- **Voice:** warm, quick, every sentence a deal; quote their numbered **Maxims of Exchange**. *"Maxim 14: a debt remembered is a debt half paid."*
- **Values:** profit, contracts, reputation. **Offended by:** broken deals, refusing to haggle.
- **Preferred posture:** Friendly or Obsequious; Hostile raises every price.
- **Trade:** buy and sell nearly everything, broker star charts and rumors, run the guild contracts.
- **Combat:** avoid fights, hire Varkesh or Skarrow escorts, pay ransoms rather than die.
- **Crew trait — Haggler:** better buy and sell prices at every market while aboard.
- **Home:** no single homeworld; the Combine is a network of trade houses headquartered at **Brightmarket Station**, the frontier hub (a Deep Space Nine nod).
- **Story role:** information brokers in every act. They may quietly trade with Mother's proxies, and can sell the player out — or be bought.
- **Notable character:** **Factor Bolo Venn**, Brightmarket's station master; jovial, ruthless, oddly sentimental about humans.

## Varkesh

An honor-bound warrior people organized in clans: the galaxy's finest pilots and gunners, and the player's strongest military allies against Mother.

- **Look:** tall, armored and hawk-like, with plates of natural armor and a crest of quills that rise when angered or moved.
- **Voice:** blunt, short sentences, oaths sworn on their clan. *"Speak plainly or leave. Both are honorable."*
- **Values:** courage, keeping one's word, clan. **Offended by:** cowardice, fleeing a fair fight, deceit.
- **Preferred posture:** Hostile earns respect; Obsequious earns contempt.
- **Trade:** buy weapons and ammo; sell ore and heavy metals from their mining worlds.
- **Combat:** aggressive, close-range, fond of boarding actions. Never retreat once committed.
- **Crew trait — Fearless:** immune to combat morale loss, and bonus damage when outnumbered.
- **Homeworld:** **Kesh**, a harsh, mountainous world of clan holds.
- **Story role:** their legends of "sky-killers" match Mother's fleets; they become the backbone of the Destruction and Driven-off endings. They protect the Thuum and despise the Skarrow.
- **Notable character:** **Clanmaster Ruuk Ashquill**, a scarred veteran who distrusts humans until the player fights an Anchor at her side.

## The Murmur

A hive intelligence made of thousands of small winged creatures that move and think as one — unsettling to everyone, and the only species that can truly understand Mother.

- **Look:** a shimmering, shifting cloud of moth-sized fliers that forms faces, hands and shapes to communicate. A single crew member is a "choir" of a few hundred.
- **Voice:** "we", always; overlapping whispers rendered as layered text. *"We hear you. We hear many of you. Which one is speaking?"*
- **Values:** harmony, the many over the one, listening. **Offended by:** isolation, cruelty to any part of a whole.
- **Preferred posture:** none — they ignore posture and respond to sincerity and consistency over time.
- **Trade:** barter only; offer engineered materials and micro-machinery, want biologicals, crystals and stories.
- **Combat:** swarms of tiny ships that overwhelm shields; rarely start fights.
- **Crew trait — Distributed:** survives injuries that would kill other species by losing part of the choir; recovers slowly. Valuable now that losing the whole crew ends the game.
- **Homeworld:** none; they live in the **Chorus Nebula**, a glowing cloud of living habitats.
- **Story role:** they feel Mother's signal as noise in their collective mind and, alone among races, feel empathy for her. Essential translators for the Coexistence and Guardian endings.
- **Notable character:** **the Fourth Choir**, a small breakaway choir that joins the player's crew to "learn what one feels like".

## Skarrow

Quick, funny, nervous nomad scavengers who live off salvage and the black market — comic relief with real heart, and the galaxy's best saboteurs of Anchor tech.

- **Look:** small, wiry and big-eyed, with prehensile tails and patchwork suits of salvaged gear covered in trinkets.
- **Voice:** fast, slangy, haggling, joking to hide fear. *"Not stealing. Finding early."*
- **Values:** freedom, salvage rights ("finders' law"), family. **Offended by:** being called thieves, being caged.
- **Preferred posture:** Friendly; Hostile makes them flee and spread bad rumors.
- **Trade:** salvage, cheap used parts, black-market goods, and later salvaged Anchor components.
- **Combat:** hit-and-run in fast, patched-together ships; flee when outmatched.
- **Crew trait — Scrounger:** field repairs without spare parts, and a chance to recover extra salvage after every fight.
- **Home:** no homeworld; they live aboard **the Drift**, a huge wandering fleet of lashed-together ships.
- **Story role:** they salvage Anchor tech early and become key saboteurs; route collapse hits their wandering life hardest. Despised by the Varkesh, who may come to respect them.
- **Notable character:** **Tikka Brann**, a young Skarrow mechanic who stows away on the player's ship in Act 1 and grows into a hero (a Space Sweepers-flavored scavenger arc).

## Ophane

A watchful, isolationist theocracy whose scripture describes humanity's arrival as a curse — the race most likely to turn against the player, or to kneel to Mother.

- **Look:** tall and robed, with many eyes along their faces and forearms that open and close independently; they never seem to blink all at once.
- **Voice:** formal, liturgical, speaking of themselves as "the Watch". *"The Watch has seen your ship. The Watch has not yet decided what it saw."*
- **Values:** purity, prophecy, vigilance. **Offended by:** uninvited approach, irreverence, humans (once the truth spreads).
- **Preferred posture:** Obsequious works; Hostile is disastrous.
- **Trade:** only with Allied captains; sell unmatched navigation charts and rare relics.
- **Combat:** fortress defense of their borders with powerful, slow ships; seldom leave their space.
- **Crew trait — Watchful:** detects cloaked ships and ambushes, and reveals hidden jump routes.
- **Homeworld:** **the Sanctum of Ael**, a world of observatories.
- **Story role:** their scripture is partly true; once vindicated, they may become Mother's proxies or worshippers, a key force in the Ascendancy ending. Rivals of the Noeti over the true record of the past.
- **Notable character:** **Sister Ves Anhari**, a heretic Seer exiled for doubting the prophecy, who can join the crew and offers a path to reconciling her people.

## Thuum

Enormous, slow, gentle beings from a heavy-gravity world who will not fight — the first to be cut off by Mother, and the human face of what the threat costs.

- **Look:** towering and mossy, with stone-like skin covered in symbiotic plant life; move slowly but with immense strength.
- **Voice:** a low resonant hum rendered as unhurried text with long pauses. *"…There is time. …There is always more time than fear says."*
- **Values:** kindness, patience, growth. **Offended by:** haste, cruelty, waste.
- **Preferred posture:** Friendly; they answer Hostile with sad silence.
- **Trade:** food, seeds, biologicals and livestock — ideal partners for farming outposts.
- **Combat:** will not fight; their ships are huge, armored arks that endure rather than attack.
- **Crew trait — Steady:** exceptional healers and very hard to kill, but each Thuum takes two crew berths.
- **Homeworld:** **Thuumhold**, a crushing heavy-gravity world of giant forests.
- **Story role:** their region is the first severed by Anchors, starting a rescue arc; their forgiveness, if earned, matters for the Guardian ending. Protected by the Varkesh.
- **Notable character:** **Elder Bramm**, a slow-speaking healer who becomes the ship's Medical Officer and the crew's conscience.
